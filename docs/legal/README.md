# Aangan hosted: legal drafts

**DRAFTS. Not legal advice. A lawyer must review these before the first paying family.**
Written in plain English on purpose: families should be able to read them.

| File | What it is | Where it shows |
|---|---|---|
| `terms.md` | Terms of service between Aangan and the family admin who subscribes | Sign-up, before payment; footer link |
| `privacy.md` | Aangan's privacy notice: what Aangan itself handles and why | Sign-up; footer link; linked from every family site |
| `declaration.md` | The short declaration the family admin ticks before their site is made | Sign-up, the step before payment |

Each family's own site keeps its own privacy page (`web/public/privacy.html`) for relatives, plus the consent every
relative gives on joining (`policy_versions`). These documents sit above that.

Things to settle with the lawyer:
1. **Aangan's legal entity** (sole proprietor, LLP or company), registered address and GST position. Fill in every
   `[ENTITY]`, `[ADDRESS]`, `[EMAIL]` and `[GRIEVANCE OFFICER]`.
2. **Roles under the DPDP Act 2023 and Rules 2025.** These drafts treat Aangan as the Data Fiduciary for the service it
   runs (it decides how the service works and earns from it), with the family admin deciding what the family puts in.
   The lawyer should confirm this, or whether the admin is the fiduciary and Aangan the processor.
3. **Where data is kept.** Family databases are created in the Mumbai region; email and some infrastructure providers
   process data outside India. Check the Rules on transfers.
4. **Children.** Verifiable parental consent: the site already makes a parent consent for anyone under 18 and approve
   what they add. Check this meets the Rules.
5. **Breach reporting** timelines to the Data Protection Board and to affected people.
6. **Refunds, cancellation, auto-renewal notices** (RBI e-mandate rules for UPI AutoPay and cards).
7. **Consumer law**: grievance redressal under the Consumer Protection (E-Commerce) Rules.
